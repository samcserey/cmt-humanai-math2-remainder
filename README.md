## Thesis

A mathematical sketch generated by an autoregressive language model is not a literal symbolic derivation. It is a stochastic prediction conditioned on prior context and learned parameters. The relevant object is therefore not only the classical approximation error of a finite-order expansion, but also a generative residual induced by the model’s predictive mechanism itself.

Let the mathematical target be \(T\), and let the observed sketch be \(S\). Classical analysis writes

\[
T = P_n(c) + R_n(c),
\]

where \(P_n(c)\) is the finite-order approximation and \(R_n(c)\) is the classical remainder. This captures the truncation or asymptotic error of the analytic expansion.

But an AI-generated sketch is not merely the evaluation of a fixed approximation. For a context \(c\), the model defines a predictive distribution

\[
\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid c),
\]

and the generated output is obtained by applying a decoding rule \(D\) under randomness \(\xi\):

\[
S = D(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid c), \xi).
\]

Therefore the observed artifact contains two distinct sources of discrepancy:

\[
S = T + E_{\mathrm{classical}} + R_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]

Here:
- \(E_{\mathrm{classical}}\) is the ordinary approximation or truncation error;
- \(R_{\mathrm{gen}}\) is the generative residual induced by model approximation, decoding randomness, and the compounding of local token-level uncertainty into a globally mis-specified argument.

This is the essential point: the validity of a classical bound does not imply that the generative remainder has vanished. A model may reproduce the correct leading-order behavior while still carrying a nonzero residual because the predictive process is not exact symbolic reasoning.

## Balanced Accounting

The observable artifact should therefore be represented as

\[
\text{Observed sketch}
=
\text{Classical main term}
+
O(\text{classical error})
+
R_{\mathrm{gen}}.
\]

This keeps both error sources explicit.

- The classical term remains independently verifiable.
- The generative residual is not silently absorbed.
- The sketch is treated as a stochastic prediction, not as a theorem certified by exact symbolic execution.

## Why the Generative Remainder Matters

In an autoregressive system, each token is generated conditionally on the previously generated context. A proof-like output is therefore not a deterministic function of the context in the same way as an explicit mathematical derivation. Local predictive mistakes can accumulate across steps, and the final text may look coherent while still carrying a residual mismatch between the generated argument and the underlying mathematical truth.

This matters even when the model is large. Increasing nominal parameter count does not guarantee a vanishing error floor. What matters is effective representational capacity, not merely nominal parameter size. In large models, weakly active, dormant, or poorly constrained directions can absorb mismatch, producing a nonzero asymptotic residual floor.

Thus, even when the nominal parameter count increases and the leading behavior appears to improve, the effective residual need not vanish. It may plateau at a nonzero floor unless the model class, data, and optimization regime are sufficiently expressive to realize the target exactly.

In other words:

- a model may approximate the right leading-order behavior,
- a classical bound may remain valid,
- yet the model-generated output still carries a nonzero generative error.

The residual therefore belongs in the accounting, not in the footnotes.

## Protocol Rules

1. Cite originating sources exactly.
2. Verify classical estimates independently.
3. Retain \(R_{\mathrm{gen}}\) as an explicit term.
4. Report the balanced decomposition so both error sources remain visible.
5. State clearly that the validity of a classical bound does not imply the generative remainder vanished.

## Origin

Developed in real-time HumanAI co-learning (Sam C. Serey & xAI Grok) under Chaos Musical Theory (CMT°). The project is complementary to classical mathematical verification: it does not replace classical remainder analysis, but ensures that the model-generated residual remains visible and explicit.

Math orders the approximation by refusing to drop the residual that matters.

## Summary

This repository argues for a disciplined accounting principle:

\[
\text{Generated mathematical output}
=
\text{classical object}
+
\text{classical error}
+
\text{generative residual}.
\]

The goal is not to deny the value of classical analysis, but to ensure that AI-assisted mathematical reasoning is not mistaken for exact reasoning merely because its leading terms look familiar or its bounds remain valid. The residual is not decorative; it is part of the model’s actual output.
